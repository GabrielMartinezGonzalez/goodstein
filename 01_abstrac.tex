In this work, we study Goodstein's theorem, focusing on its interpretation within Peano arithmetic. We also study hydras, mathematical objects whose behavior can likewise be entirely described within Peano arithmetic and which satisfy a theorem analogous to Goodstein's theorem.
Around these two results, we develop the theories of first-order logic, computable functions, ordinal theory, non-standard models of a first-order language, model indicators, and $\alpha$-large sets, in order to prove that both results are independent of the axioms of Peano arithmetic; that is, they cannot be proved within this formal system.
This work is a compilation of results intended to provide complete and self-contained proofs of both unprovability results by developing a model in which neither theorem holds, as well as to explore the connections between them.
The work brings together classical results from model theory, along with a wide range of results concerning the study of Peano arithmetic, featuring the contributions of mathematicians such as Ketonen, Solovay, Kirby, and Paris.
